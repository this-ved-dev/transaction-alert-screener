What regulators expect, and where banks stumbled

Initial finding · to validate with you
Requirement

Cash over $10,000 per person per business day, all accounts and branches combined, needs a CTR.

Detect

Where it went wrong

✓ FinCEN
Requirement

A deposit near $10,000 is not suspicious on its own. Look for a pattern that suggests intent.

✓ Interagency SAR FAQ, Oct 2025

Triage

Where it went wrong

USAA. 90,000 alerts went unreviewed after a new system launched without a pilot.

✓ FinCEN
Requirement

Record why you chose not to file. A short, concise statement is enough.

✓ SAR FAQ, Oct 2025

Decide

Where it went wrong

Blue Ridge Bank, about $3B. No written standards for closing alerts. Decisions not to file went undocumented.

✓ OCC 2024
Requirement

File a SAR within 30 days of initial detection, or 60 if no suspect is known. Keep it confidential.

✓ 31 CFR 1020.320

File

Where it went wrong

Bank of America. A backlog of about 200,000 cases, and thresholds judged not appropriate.

✓ OCC
Requirement

Keep records for 5 years, and tell the board about filings.

✓ 31 CFR 1010.430 · 12 CFR 21.11

Retain and report

Where it went wrong

Blue Ridge Bank. The board had no view of the alert backlog.

✓ OCC 2024

Most of these penalties trace back to volume and missing records, not to a pattern nobody spotted.

What usually slows these projects down

Getting the data

Nightly batch files, and some cores charge per dataset.

Transaction codes

Each core codes cash, wire and ACH its own way.

Customer vs account

Files keyed by account hide structuring across one customer's accounts.

Vendor review

Third-party risk review and a SOC 2 report. ✓ OCC 2023-17

Switching over

Examiners expect old and new to run side by side first.
